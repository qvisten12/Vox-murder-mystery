## Narration (exact words to be spoken)

February 28th, 1986. Stockholm.
A man walks home from the cinema with his wife. No bodyguards. No car.
He is the Prime Minister of Sweden.

That morning, Olof Palme sent his security detail home. He wanted one normal Friday night.

At 11:21 p.m., on Sveavägen, a man steps out of the dark behind him.
One shot. In the back. At point-blank range.
A second bullet grazes his wife, Lisbet.

The gunman runs up the stairs of Tunnelgatan — and disappears.

The police don't seal off the city. The national alarm comes hours too late.
The revolver is never found.

In 1988, Lisbet picks a man out of a lineup: Christer Pettersson.
He's convicted — then freed on appeal. No weapon. No motive.

Over a hundred people confess. None of them did it.

Then, in 2020, after thirty-four years, prosecutors name a new suspect:
Stig Engström — the "Skandia Man," an office worker who said he was a witness at the scene.
But Engström had died twenty years earlier. He was never charged. The case was closed.

The gun is still missing. No one was ever convicted.

So — was it the Skandia Man… or someone who walked away that night and was never found?
