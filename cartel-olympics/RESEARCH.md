## The story as told (Mauricio "Mau" Morales's claims)
- 9 Feb 2023: kidnapped in Mexico City's San Rafael neighbourhood while leading migrants he met at a bus station; taken in vans.
- Woke "on the floor of a dank, windowless room with a single mattress and a bucket in one corner." Says he was tortured for days.
- Boss "Don Paco" of La Unión Tepito saw an Olympic-rings tattoo on his wrist ("I know who you are").
- Claimed to be an Olympic runner (Beijing, London, Rio).
- Secret annual tournament among Mexico's biggest cartels: soccer, flag football and boxing, with bosses betting.
- Mau coached and played flag football. "If you win, you live. If you lose, you die."
- His team won in overtime and he got a gold medal. He was dumped on a sidewalk at dawn in his underwear.
- Corroborating witness: "Mamers", said to be a reformed hit man.

## Who bought it
- Dec 2024: Robert Reynolds, a Las Vegas lawyer and talent manager, emails The Atlantic: "Massive story".
- Actor Michael Peña was reportedly interested in playing Mau, on condition that a reputable outlet vet and publish the story ("based on a true story").
- The Atlantic reporter spent about 8 months on it, including a trip to Mexico City with security.

## What the reporting found
- No record of Mau with the Mexican or Colombian Olympic committees.
- His "Olympics" photo shows Italian runner Pietro Arese. Mau later admitted: "I didn't run."
- His 400k Instagram followers showed almost no engagement. Photos with Tyson, Messi and Kofi Annan were fabricated.
- A UN email signed "Filippo Grandi" was forged, and "human-rights expert James Winston" was fictional.
- 9 Feb 2023 was the day he was **arrested for fraud**. He spent about 18 months in an ordinary men's prison in south Mexico City.
- The fraud: via the Mexican office of the Non-Violence Project Foundation, he took about $700,000 from union leader Alejandro Martínez,
  using fake Beatles memorabilia and fake Yoko Ono emails. He fled to Europe and was returned to Mexico.
- "Mamers" was a fellow inmate awaiting trial for fraud and bike theft, not a hit man.
- Mau's response: the documents were "doctored" and "He was actually the victim here."
- Per Barstool (unconfirmed): after learning the truth, Peña liked it even more and "The movie is officially back on."
